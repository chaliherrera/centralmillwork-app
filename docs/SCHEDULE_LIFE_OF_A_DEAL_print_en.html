Design principles

The seven rules that govern the entire system. If any one of them is rejected, the design changes.

#PrincipleWhat it meansNotes / objections
P1The target date is sacredThe delivery date committed to the client is never recalculated automatically. Internal dates and the projection are recalculated. When the projection passes the target date, the project turns red — the date does not move. Moving it is a recorded human decision: who, when, why, how many days.
P2No milestone closes on a claim — it closes on evidenceThere is no “it’s done” checkbox. Every milestone closes because the artifact that proves it appeared (a file, a submittal, a client click, a station move), and the system sets the date, not the person.
P3The record is a by-product of the workThe drawing submittal goes out from the system, so the date records itself. If someone has to perform an extra task just to report, that design is wrong and will be abandoned within three weeks.
P4Progress is counted, not estimatedNo human-typed “% complete”. Progress is evidenced sub-milestones completed over total. If it cannot be counted, it is not displayed.
P5Two clocksBackward from the delivery date: the deadline for each milestone. Forward from actual events: where we are going to land. The gap between them is float. Negative float = risk of missing the date.
P6The system pushes early, it does not explain lateA schedule that only absorbs delays is a scoreboard of losses. Every milestone approaching its deadline alerts its owner beforehand, and automatically reminds the client when the ball is in their court.
P7Whoever arrives last holds the ballWhen a milestone has several predecessors, its actual start is the latest of them. The system names which one it was and how many days it cost. No argument.

The journey

CONTRACT → ENGINEERING → MATERIALS → PRODUCTION → QC → SHIPPING → INSTALL → COMPLETED

The target delivery date is entered when the project is created (Contract phase). There the system calculates backward and says whether it is born achievable or already at risk — that calculation is the feasibility check. The operational day zero is contract execution (C-03).

Assembly is not a phase. In the official document it appears only as a shop stage inside Manufacture (p.33) and Quality Control (p.34). Station-level detail is already tracked by the Production module to the minute.

Risk model

StatusMeaning
GREENFloat ≥ 3 working days
YELLOWFloat between 0 and 3 days
REDNegative float: already eating into the delivery date
GRAYNot yet applicable (predecessors unmet)
Risk management as a discipline. Each phase has a schedule-review milestone (E-12 in Engineering, M-01 in Procurement) whose explicit purpose is to verify whether the deadline can be met. It is the operational face of the two-clocks model: it does not wait for the delay, it anticipates it.

Attribution of lost days

Every milestone that closes late records who the delay belongs to:

Client · GC · Vendor · Estimating · Engineering · Procurement · Production · Logistics · Field · Finance

That running total is the report that answers “why do we deliver late?” at year end with numbers instead of opinions — and it is the documented argument in front of GCs and clients.

Warning: the first report will be uncomfortable. It is a diagnosis of the process, not a control on people.

Why it is achievable now

All of that data exists or is derived from what the app tracks today (projects, items, progress by station, due dates). It is one of the first things to come out of the engine, and it replaces a weekly manual task for the PM.

Client portal

Tokenized link access, one per contact — no accounts to create. Every action is attributed to a specific person.

The client can

  • See their project timeline by milestone, with status and dates
  • See what the project is waiting on and who holds the ball
  • Approve / approve as noted / reject: shop drawings, samples, V/E, early procurement
  • Give the final sign-off
  • See fabrication and installation progress photos
  • Download contract, approved drawings, BOL, shipping list

Automatic to the client

“Shop drawings have been under review for 12 days. Each additional day pushes the October 15 delivery.”

Turns the “waiting on the client” black hole into a data point with a date, a time and an owner. The client does NOT see costs, margins, budgets, vendors or pricing.

Why this order

  • Stage 1 starts where the data already exists, and the 3WLA gives immediate value
  • Stage 2 is the largest gain for the least code
  • Stage 3 is the biggest, and lands once the engine is proven
  • Stage 5 (Contract intake) comes late because the schedule already works starting from the contract, even if its entry is manual at first
Honest warning: the estimate assumes consistency. If interrupted for weeks to handle production, it stretches. And it does not include adoption time, which in the office areas may weigh more than the development itself.

What this review needs to produce

  • Durations. The ones in this document are estimates. Correct them with each area’s owner.
  • Owners. The official document names 13 roles; the system today has 9 and they do not match. They need to be mapped.
  • Milestones that are missing, unnecessary, or that don’t work this way in practice.
  • Granularity. Per item / area with rollup to the project (defined) — validate against this map.
  • Working calendar. Working days, Central Millwork holidays, shutdown weeks.
  • Phased delivery. Projects delivered by floor or by area with separate dates.
  • Projects without installation. The document contemplates them (p.37). The chain would close at S-04.
  • Change orders. The milestone was removed from the journey in Rev 1; how they impact the target date still needs defining.
Closing note. This map does not invent a process: it transcribes the one Central Millwork already has written down, and turns it into something the system can calculate. What is missing is not the idea — it is that this schedule stops living in a spreadsheet nobody updates and starts finding out what is actually happening.
