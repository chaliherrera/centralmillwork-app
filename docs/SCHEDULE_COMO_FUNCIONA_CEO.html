The one idea

Every project has one delivery date. The system works backward from it to figure out what must happen and by when — and it warns before a deadline slips, instead of explaining after.

Today this lives in spreadsheets that never find out what actually happened. The system connects the plan to reality.

5

Accountability — who holds the ball, and where days are lost

  • At any moment, the system shows which phase is active and who it's waiting on.
  • When something finishes late, it records who the delay belongs to.
At year end, one report answers "why did we deliver late?" with numbers, not opinions:
Client · GC · Vendor · Estimating · Engineering · Procurement · Production · Logistics · Field · Finance
6

The client watches their own project

  • A private link — no account needed — shows the client their timeline and what it's waiting on.
  • They approve drawings, samples and sign-off right there; the date and person are captured automatically.
  • Automatic reminders when the ball is in their court — with the impact on the delivery date spelled out.
The client never sees costs, margins, vendors or pricing.
This turns "waiting on the client" from a black hole into a dated, accountable fact.
7

What we build first — honest scope

Stage 1 — already underway
The engine + the phases the system already measures (Materials, Production, QC) + the auto-generated 3-Week Lookahead. Half the journey, live, from the start.
Then, in order
Client portal → Engineering (drawings, CNC, release) → Field & Install on mobile → Contract intake → Financial close-out.

The office areas that work outside the system today (Estimating, Engineering, Field) are where most delays happen. Bringing that work in is the real decision behind this project — not the software.
